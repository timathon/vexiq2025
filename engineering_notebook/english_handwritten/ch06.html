Chapter 6: Gen 1 Verification & Competition Analysis

System Testing & Competition Analysis

This chapter records the performance testing of "Gen 1" after assembly, and its real-world performance in the "WRC Beijing Qualifier" and "Beijing Urban Competition."

Through detailed logs (June 30 - Aug 12), we review technical bottlenecks in movement, grabbing stability, and autonomous control, along with our engineering fixes.

Core Task: Evaluate the Gen 1 design, verify 91/110 point strategies, and provide data for the development of Gen 2.

6.1 Unit Test: Chassis & Intake System

In early tests, we found the pneumatic claw slipped on smooth Pins, and the chassis had significant drift.

Date: June 30, 2025 | Location: Jianwai SOHO

  • Issue A: Chassis Drift
    Phenomenon: The robot couldn't drive straight, drifting left or right, causing big errors over long distances.
    Diagnosis: Power mismatch between the two drive motors.
    Fix: Motor Replacement - Tested for motor consistency, replaced them with a matched pair, and updated firmware.
  • Issue B: Gripper Slip
    Phenomenon: Pins weren't held securely; they would shift or fall during 180-degree flips due to centrifugal force.
    Fix: Added Friction - Added rubber bands to the inner claw for better grip. Structural Tweak - Adjusted cylinder limits so force is applied directly to the Pin's center of gravity.

6.2 Function Test: Stacking & Hooking

In practice for "two-layer stacking" and "Beam dragging," we fixed hook slippage and stability issues, letting the driver complete complex moves smoothly.

Date: July 6, 2025 | Location: Jianwai SOHO

  • Issue A: Beam Unhooking
    Phenomenon: The hook would slip off when dragging the Beam.
    Fix: Optimized Hook Angle - Re-bent the hook's end so it locks deeper into the Beam structure.
  • Issue B: Uncoordinated Moves
    Phenomenon: "Dragging + Flipping" at the same time caused the robot to lose balance and drift.
    Fix: Space Re-allocation - Repositioned the chassis, intake, hook, and flip components to better balance the robot's weight.

6.3 System Integration: Semi-Auto Logic

To help the driver during intense matches, we added sensor-assisted logic based on July 6th feedback.

Software Update: Auto-Grab

Background (July 6th Recap): Driver had to do too much manual micro-adjusting, leading to fatigue and errors late in the match.

Technical Fix: Integrated the front Distance Sensor.

  • Logic: When Distance < 50mm (Pin detected) → Automatically trigger the claw to close.
  • Benefit: Upgraded from "visual judgment" to "sensor trigger," saving about 0.5s per grab.

6.4 Simulation: Teamwork Drills

Simulation Match Info

Time: July 16, 2025

Content: Full-process simulated match

Purpose: Expose non-mechanical teamwork issues

Just a month before the Beijing City Competition, we did a full simulation. It exposed some serious teamwork gaps:

Issues Exposed

  • Strategy Confusion: Members weren't sure of routes and couldn't explain them well to alliance partners.
  • Messy Handovers: During the 25-35s driver switch, the team got in each other's way, causing the robot to stop unexpectedly.
  • Poor Records: Pre-match meeting notes were incomplete, missing rule details.

Actions Taken

  • Description Training: Members must be able to clearly explain "Far" and "Near" route logic.
  • Standardized Zones: Defined exactly where each driver stands and how to pass the controller.
  • Intensive Drills: Practiced "perfect timing" handovers and pre-match strategy communication.

6.6 Specialized Training: Road to Shijiazhuang

To prep for the Shijiazhuang event, we did high-intensity drills focused on high-scoring moves.

Date: Aug 12, 2025 | Location: Jianwai SOHO

Focus: Preparing for the Shijiazhuang Match

Core Training Goals

  1. Push for maximum points!
  2. Perfect the angle for sending Pins to the Center Island.
  3. Improve High Tower accuracy and release timing.
  4. Practice Triangle Zone Pin delivery angles.
  5. Driver 1 must call out time for Driver 2.
  6. Repeatedly drill "91 point Low Tower" route.

Review & Summary

  • Issue 1: Slow Flip
    Phenomenon: Waiting until we reached the zone to flip the wrist, wasting time.
    Fix: Time Tracking - Monitor and force the flip to happen while the robot is moving.
  • Issue 2: Center Island Misses
    Phenomenon: Often had to readjust position to drop Pins correctly.
    Fix: Visual Cues - Use field lines as reference points for the approach route.

6.6 Specialized Training (Cont.)

Review & Summary (Cont.)

  • Issue 3: High Tower Beam Toppling
    Phenomenon: Beam often fell over during release on the high tower.
    Fix: Timing Drills - Practiced micro-timing for the claw release to find the sweet spot.
  • Issue 4: Triangle Zone Difficulty
    Phenomenon: Either knocked Pins over or struggled to get them in.
    Fix: Data Tweaking - Adjusted delivery angles and raised arm height in the program.

Chapter 6 Summary: Verification & Iteration

The Beijing Urban Competition proved Gen 1 was a successful "proof-of-concept." It validated our pneumatic design but showed our hardware limits.

Gen 2 R&D Plan

Based on our data, here's the plan for the next version:

  1. Faster Chassis: Speed up the gear ratio without losing control.
  2. High Tower Optimization: Longer arm for easier release and better timing logic.
  3. Triangle Zone: Add a dedicated mechanism to score in Triangles.
  4. Coordination: Better alignment between Driver and Loader.

Decision: Gen 1 is officially retired. Build for Gen 2 starts immediately!
