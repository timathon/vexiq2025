Gen 3: Striving for Excellence

Chapter 8: R&D and Competition Season

After enduring the trials of Gen 2, we were thrilled by the progress but also aware of the stability and thermal bottlenecks during high-intensity, long-duration matches. These "imperfections" became the strongest motivation for Gen 3.

This chapter records how Gen 3 underwent structural optimizations to address core pain points and details its performance in key season events.

Core Mission: Build a faster, more flexible, and more stable all-around machine and validate its performance in the field.

8.1 Gen 3 Design: Improvements from the Field

Based on issues exposed during the Asia Open and other events, we established core goals for Gen 3 to achieve comprehensive performance gains.

Gen 3 Design Goals

  1. Chassis Clearance: Completely solve the friction between the chassis gears and the "Loading Beam."
  2. Triangle Efficiency: Optimize the Aligner structure to reduce operation time in various scenarios.
  3. Stacking Stability: Upgrade pin support from "Point-based" to "Surface-based" to prevent high-level tip-overs.
  4. Workflow Automation: Refine code for smoother transitions between grabbing and stacking sequences.

8.2 Mech Opt: Chassis

1. Gear Shift Upwards

Action: Moved the entire drivetrain gear assembly up by one unit height.

Result: Eliminated physical friction with the "Loading Beam." Gen 3 moves through the loading zone much more smoothly and faster than Gen 2, saving precious seconds.


Old Chassis (Lower gear position)


New Chassis (Gears shifted up)

8.2 Mech Opt: Aligner

2. Semi-Retracted Structure

Action: Changed the aligners from fully retracted to a "semi-retracted" state and swapped long pneumatic rods for short ones.

Result: Maintained flexibility while significantly increasing response speed, making scoring in the Triangle Zone much faster.


Aligner Top View

Aligner Bottom View

8.2 Mech Opt: Stacking

3. Surface-based Support

Action: Upgraded the "point-based" support structure for the bottom pin to a "surface-based" support.

Result: The new structure holds the bottom pin much more securely, drastically reducing the risk of high stacks tipping over due to robot movement or collisions.


Gen 3: Surface-based Support

8.3 Coding & Automation: Seeking Fluidity

Mech optimizations paved the way for higher-level automation. We focused on chaining independent functions into an "One-Button" scoring workflow.

Core Code Optimizations

  • Refined Triangle Control: Used the semi-retracted aligners to write faster Enter-Score-Exit sequences, minimizing driver burden.
  • Optimized Multi-Stacking: Tweaked the lift curves and flip timing for high stacks, reducing wait times for a seamless flow.

Training Results

Testing confirmed that Gen 3's automation reached competition requirements for stability and speed. Continuous operation success rates are significantly higher than Gen 2, even under high-pressure simulations.

8.8 VEX Asia Open (2)

III. Core Issue: Triangle Instability

1. Symptoms & Fixes

We hit a recurring issue where "Triangle cycles failed due to unstable grip height."

  • Root Cause: Post-event analysis found imprecise variables for the lift motor.
  • Quick Fix: The driver had to manually compensate for the angle, costing 2-3s per operation.
  • Field Adjustments: We boosted the lift height variable (`PIN=5`) by 3 degrees, which helped but didn't solve the underlying issue.

8.8 VEX Asia Open (3)

8.11 Season Summary: Towards a New Journey

From Imperfection to Excellence

Looking back at the season, from the initial exploration of Gen 1 to the battlefield trials of Gen 2, and finally to the maturity of Gen 3, every step was full of challenges and growth. The "imperfections" of Gen 2 exposed our weaknesses, but those same weaknesses guided the R&D for Gen 3, ultimately leading us to the podium.

Future Outlook

The VEX competition is endless, and technical iterations never stop. While celebrating our victories, we are already thinking about the next generation of machines.

  • Stronger Resilience: Future matches may be more aggressive; we need to enhance the robot's structural impact resistance.
  • Smarter Automation: We aim to introduce advanced sensors (like Vision) to allow the robot to autonomously identify field elements.
  • Modular Design: Explore more modular systems to quickly swap functional components based on different field conditions.

Continuous improvement, never stopping. The journey of 2276A continues.
