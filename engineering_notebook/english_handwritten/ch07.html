Chapter 7: The Evolution of Gen 2

Design Iteration: Gen 2

This chapter records the full journey of developing Gen 2, based on the drift, reach, and accuracy issues identified in Gen 1.

Key upgrades: Optimized chassis speed (1.5x ratio), integrated 4X beam intake, physical limit tuning for the lift, pneumatic aligners, and one-button automated macros.

Core Task: Build a more capable, flexible, and stable robot for high-level competition.

7.1 Iteration Start: Fixing Version 1.0

Based on our Beijing Urban Competition data, we set four technical goals for Gen 2:

Gen 2 Design Goals

  1. Chassis Stability: Fix the high-speed drift of the 2x chassis by tuning it to a 1.5x effective ratio via software.
  2. Intake Precision: Use an integrated 4X beam to fix friction points and reduce Pin offset during flips.
  3. Lift Tuning: Recalculate arm length and rotation for the Center Tower to balance height and stability.
  4. Triangle Zone Efficiency: Introduce movable aligners and automation for fast, precise entry into Triangle Goals.

7.2 Optimization 1: Chassis & Motion Control

Fix 1: Gear Relocation (Ground Clearance)

Pain Point: Gen 1 gears were too low, scraping against Loading Beams. We moved the gear assembly up to eliminate ground interference.

Fix 2: Speed Dampening (Motion Control)

Improvement: Reduced the gear ratio from 2x to 1.5x. We also added a 0.75x software stability coefficient to the drive output.

Result: Much smoother movement. It no longer scatters Pins at high speed, making pre-grab positioning far more accurate.

7.3 Optimization 2: Integrated Intake & Hook

1. Integrated 4X Beam Intake

Action: Switched to an integrated 4X beam and wrapped rubber bands for extra friction.

Advantage: Fixed friction points ensure Pins don't shift during flips. Grab success rate is now 95%.

2. Hook Arch Optimization

Action: Adjusted the hook angle to center it on the Beam and optimized physical limits to prevent vibration-induced slippage.

Advantage: Dual-function hook for both Beams and Pins provides a solid foundation for lifting and stacking.

7.4 Opt. 3: Lift Geometry & Height Redundancy

Diagnosis: Height vs. Stability Imbalance

In version 1.0, the robot struggled to reach the second layer of the High Tower. The short arm caused high motor load and severe wobbling, often toppling the stack.

Improvement: Geometric Reconstruction

1. Lengthened Arm: Increased the lift arm by 2.5 inches. While this increased torque load, we used a 1:5 gear reduction to compensate, gaining significant reach.

2. Retained Precision: Kept the physical limits for the Pin base to ensure grabbing accuracy wasn't sacrificed for height.

Evaluation:

Stacking height now comfortably meets requirements with much better stability.

7.5 Optimization 4: Pneumatic Aligner System

Background: Solving Entry Interference

Fixed aligners often got stuck on the Triangle Goal edges. This fix uses pneumatics to clear obstacles and speed up scoring.

Core Solution: Pneumatic Retraction + Automation

  • Structure: Added a cylinder module to auto-lift/retract the aligner near edges, avoiding physical friction.
  • One-Key Macro: Developed the Score_In_Triangle() function to automate the "lift-align-reverse" sequence.

Efficiency Gains

  • 1. Smooth Action: Complex alignment is now a single automated button press.
  • 2. Speed Boost: Delivery time dropped from 6s to 2.5s, allowing for 2 more stacks per match.

  • Overall: Gen 2 fixed Gen 1's stability issues and used hardware-software synergy to go from "just running" to "winning."

7.10-7.13 WRC North China Regional (Shijiazhuang)

Tournament Highlights:

  • Stats: 277 high score, 17th place final rank.
  • Success: First prize award (qualified for nationals).
  • Crisis: Motors overheated under high load; snapped pins required emergency field repairs.
  • Lessons: Need dedicated cooling (spray) and a mandatory spares checklist (motors, pins, tubes).

Decision: While Gen 2 is strong, Shijiazhuang proved it has cooling and stability limits for long events. We must start Gen 3 immediately to prepare for nationals.

Chapter 7 Summary: The Imperfect Gen 2

This chapter recorded Gen 2 from design to real matches. We fixed Gen 1's pain points and boosted performance with pneumatics and automation.

However, Gen 2 isn't the finish line. Overheating and emergency failures in Shijiazhuang showed us we need even more reliability for long schedules.

Next Steps: Gen 2's "imperfections" are our motivation. We're launching Gen 3 to tackle autonomous mode challenges and explore a completely new mechanical layout.
